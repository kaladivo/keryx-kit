Half of our support questions are some version of "I stepped in something. Are my Ramblers dead?" They almost never are. Here is what we do in the workshop.

**1. Let it dry.** Wet suede smears. Stuff the shoes with newspaper and leave them overnight, away from the radiator. Heat makes suede stiff and patchy.

**2. Brush in one direction.** Use a suede brush, or a clean dry toothbrush. Short strokes, one way, to lift the nap and knock off dried mud.

**3. Erase the marks.** A suede eraser, or a plain white pencil eraser, takes off shiny scuffs and small stains. Rub gently, then brush again.

**4. Steam, brush, protect.** For stubborn marks, hold the shoe over a steaming kettle for a few seconds, then brush. Once dry, spray a suede protector from about 20 cm away.

What not to do:

- No washing machine. We know someone who did. The shoes survived; the machine smelled like a farm for a week.
- No soap and water on suede. It leaves a ring that is harder to remove than the original stain.
- No hairdryer.

Oil or salt stains that won't move? Bring them to the pop-up, or send them in with a resole. We clean every pair that comes back to the workshop.

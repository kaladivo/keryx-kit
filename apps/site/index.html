Checking

Verifying the signatures in your browser.

The author keys come from the demo's signed targets.json. This demo channel requires both author signatures; keryx-kit channels require one. The app also checks that these keys are the ones the company's domain authorizes.

Email can't prove who sent it.

Phishers don't need to break into your systems. They only need to look like you. SPF, DKIM and DMARC help, but they are unevenly deployed, invisible to your customers, and no help against a look-alike domain.

The app fetches your feed from our servers, so like any website we see IP addresses when it checks for news. There is no name or account to tie them to.

How a message reaches your customers

  1. Your team signs

    Authors write in the browser. Their passkey unlocks the key that signs each message.

  2. Customers scan once

    They scan your QR code, confirm your domain and pick the channels they want. See what your customers read first.

  3. Only signed messages show

    The app checks every signature. If one fails, the message never appears. There is no warning to click through.

One way only: no replies and no forms, so nothing in the channel can collect a password.

What your company sets up

The owner

Approves every change to who may publish on a Trezor. The master key comes from the device and is never stored.

The device shows Keryx master key with your company's short name and asks for confirmation. Once a year the owner confirms again; the console sends reminders.

The Trezor recovery seed is the backup. Losing both the device and the seed means a new company identity, and every subscriber has to join again.

Your web admin

Uploads the join kit to an HTTPS domain you control. That domain becomes the address customers confirm.

  • /.well-known/keryx/root.json
  • /.well-known/keryx/1.root.json
  • /join/index.html

The kit's README has header snippets for nginx, Apache, Cloudflare and Netlify.

Your team

Joins by invite and registers a passkey. Then shares the join QR code in the newsletter, on receipts, on your website.

Passkeys must support the PRF extension; the console lists the browsers that work. No command line, no servers to run.

We can hold back a message. We can't write one.

Every key has one job. The keys that write messages are only ever unlocked in your authors' browsers.

What this does not cover

Our service sends the console its JavaScript. If our server were compromised, modified code could capture a key the next time someone signs. We accept this for the pilot. Each console release publishes its bundle hash, so a changed bundle is at least detectable.

We do not serve the subscriber app. It runs on its own origin and release pipeline, so a breach of our service cannot change the app your customers run.

Check the design yourself: the protocol spec, the threat model and the security policy.

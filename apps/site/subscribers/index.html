Install Keryx

Free and open source. Install it only from these two places: the app does the checking, so a fake app could show you anything.

Web app, on any phone

Open it in your browser and add it to your home screen. On an iPhone, use Safari: tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.

Check the signing certificate

Every release is signed with the same key. Tools such as AppVerifier can check this SHA-256 fingerprint before the first install:

Join a company in three steps.

The address check is the only decision Keryx asks of you. The app checks everything else.

  1. Scan the company's QR code

    In the app, tap Add a company and scan it. Or open the company's join link from a newsletter, a receipt or its website.

  2. Check the address, letter by letter

    It must be exactly the company's own website, the one you would type yourself. Not sure? Tap Cancel.

    • shoe.roguedave.codes Real
    • sh0e.roguedave.codes Look-alike

  3. Pick what to follow

    Nothing is subscribed until you tap Subscribe. Leave any time by removing the company.

The screens show Scuffwell, a demo shoe brand. You will see the company's own address.

Why you can trust what you read

Every message is checked
The app checks the company's signature before it shows a message. If the check fails, you never see it.
Nothing to give away
No email, no phone number, no account. The company keeps no list of you.
Nothing to answer
You can't reply and messages have no forms. This channel never asks for a password, a seed or a code.
Open source
The app and the protocol are public at github.com/v1b3coder/keryx.

What Keryx can't protect

The moment you join
Confirm a look-alike address, or join while an attacker controls the company's website, and you follow the attacker.
The publishing service
The company may publish through a hosted service. It can't write messages on its own, but an attacker there could hold messages back or ship altered software to steal a staff member's signing key.
Your own phone
Anyone with your unlocked phone, or malware on it, can read stored messages.
Connection data
Like any website, the servers that host the messages see your IP address when the app checks for news. There is no name or account to tie it to.

If a company's signatures ever stop checking out, the app stops showing its messages and warns you. It never asks you to scan a new code.

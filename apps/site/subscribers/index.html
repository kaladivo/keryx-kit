Follow a company without giving it your email.

A company invited you to follow its announcements in Keryx. Every message carries the company's digital signature, and the app shows only messages whose signature checks out. No email address, no phone number, no account.

Install the app

Web app, any device

The simplest way, on any phone. Open it in your browser and add it to your home screen. On an iPhone, open it in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.

Check the signing certificate

Every release is signed with the same key. Tools such as AppVerifier can check this SHA-256 fingerprint before the first install:

5E:5A:CD:AF:31:88:49:F9:6D:C4:F5:08:A0:DA:A5:A4:74:BC:60:B2:3A:2C:63:9D:33:75:85:FF:F5:E7:C8:A1

Install Keryx only from these links. The app does the checking, so a fake app could show you anything.

Join a company

  1. Scan the company's QR code

    In the Keryx app, tap Add company and scan it. Or open the company's join link from a newsletter, a receipt or its website.

  2. Check the address, letter by letter

    It must be exactly the address of the company's website, the one you would type yourself. Not sure? Tap Cancel.

    For example, if the company's address is example.com:

    example.com
    The company's real address
    examp1e.com
    Look-alike: the digit 1 instead of the letter l
    example-shop.com
    Look-alike: an extra word

  3. Pick your channels

    Nothing is subscribed until you tap Subscribe.

Checking the address is the only decision Keryx asks of you. The app checks everything else.

What Keryx protects, and what it can't

Protects

Your identity
No email, no phone number, no account. The company keeps no list of you.
Every message
The app checks each signature. If the check fails, you never see the message.
Your passwords
You cannot reply and messages have no forms, so the channel cannot collect a password, a seed phrase or a code.
Your exit
Leave any time by removing the company in the app. Nobody is told: there is no account to close.

Can't protect

The moment you join
Confirm a look-alike address, or join while an attacker controls the company's website, and you subscribe to the attacker.
The publishing service
The company may publish through a hosted service. The service cannot write messages on its own, but an attacker there could hold messages back, or ship altered software to steal the key a staff member signs with.
Your own phone
Anyone with your unlocked phone, or malware on it, can read stored messages.
Connection data
Like any website, the servers that host the company's messages see your IP address when the app checks for news. There is no name or account to tie it to.

If a company's signatures ever stop checking out, the app stops showing its messages, warns you and offers to remove it. In that case it never asks you to scan a new code. The app and the protocol are open source at github.com/v1b3coder/keryx.
